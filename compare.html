Same universe. Two formation stories. Let observations referee.

NS‑Capture and standard astrophysics can sometimes reproduce the same present-day object while telling very different stories about how it got there. This page forces both frameworks into the same format: starting assumptions → governing mathematics → predicted outputs → agreed observations.

Important: “standard astrophysics” is not one perfectly solved model. Core collapse, binary mass transfer, natal kicks, tides and common-envelope evolution are established ingredients, but some of their efficiencies remain uncertain. The fair comparison is therefore not “certain orthodoxy versus alternative theory”; it is which framework needs fewer unsupported assumptions and better reproduces independent observations quantitatively.

Why this is powerful

For an illustrative Milky Way total mass of 1.08×1012 M☉, any proposed compact-object population must fit inside the Galaxy’s measured gravitational mass. The 20-trillion-NS scenario alone contains about 2.8×1013 M☉ in neutron stars—roughly 26 times the entire measured Milky Way mass.

Microlensing also limits how much of a Galactic halo can be made of stellar-mass compact objects, although the exact constraint depends on their spatial distribution. The mass-budget test is more basic: gravity would notice them wherever they are.

5 · What observations would actually decide this?

Evidence that would materially strengthen NS‑Capture

Direct identification of a previously isolated neutron star becoming tidally captured by a normal star; a measured encounter-rate population consistent with the required HMXB/SN rate without violating Galactic mass constraints; unique pre-supernova orbital signatures of an already-existing NS inside the exploding system; and quantitative predictions for neutrinos, ejecta composition, remnant morphology and population distributions that outperform binary/core-collapse models.

Evidence that strengthens the standard framework

Pre-explosion massive binaries and stripped-star progenitors that lead into HMXBs; core-collapse neutrino signals; population synthesis that simultaneously matches HMXB numbers, orbital periods, eccentricities, spin distributions and systemic velocities; and stellar-evolution/nucleosynthesis predictions that match supernova light curves and ejecta. Common-envelope efficiency, natal-kick distributions and some mass-transfer physics remain imperfectly known.

This page deliberately labels the live calculators as formation gates, not full evolutionary simulations. A full head-to-head test eventually requires time-dependent binary evolution on the standard side and an equivalently time-dependent tidal/accretion/hydrodynamic model on the capture side.

The strongest comparison is not which story sounds more intuitive. It is which model makes the more accurate, less adjustable predictions across observations it was not designed around.
